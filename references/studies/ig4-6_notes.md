## Captions (word-build)
- **ig4 (editorial serif)**: keyword in a condensed upright display serif, off-white `#EDE6DA`, cap height 125 px
  (font ≈170 px, 39 % of width for "Alcohol"), tracking ≈ -3 %; connective words in a light grotesk 52 px directly
  under it, the last word slightly bigger (60 px). Centred at **y = 60 % of height (chest)**. No box, no stroke, soft
  shadow only. Keyword reveal = per-letter blur-to-sharp, left→right, **6-9 frames** (5.80→6.10, 1.40→1.70); small
  words appear on their speech onset with a 2-3 frame fade. Caption block holds until the shot cuts.
- **ig4 interviewer**: questions go in a **red box** (`#B5453A`, 1 px inner light rule), small white 30 px text that
  types word-by-word and the box re-sizes as lines are added (15.0-18.2). Different speaker = different treatment.
- **ig5 (one-word)**: ONE word at a time, bold geometric sans ~58 px, white on video / ink on grey paper, centred;
  word swaps every 0.2-0.4 s; exit = 2-frame blur-out (2.0-2.2).
- **ig6 (size contrast)**: small light line above ("We posted that", 34 px) + heavy keyword below ("first video",
  110 px, 60 % width); colour switches green `#2E6B3E`/white per shot. Keywords sometimes split around the head.
- B-roll captions shrink to a small italic serif (≈40 px) on the subject's chest (8.0-10.3).

## Cards / devices
- **Green grid card** (ig4 37.80-40.20, ig6 22.30-23.50): mid green `#3A9A5A` with lighter 2 px lines,

  3-4 prints, 8 px white border, rotations ±4-8°, drop shadow; the top print swaps every 0.2-0.4 s on the words.

  green sticker outline on it.

## What to take for 16:9 long-form
1. Keyword contrast in every caption (small light connective words + one big keyword; serif italic or heavy caps).
2. Blur-to-sharp reveals, 6-9 frames; nothing just "fades".
3. Interviewer vs guest get visibly different caption treatment.
4. Green grid + polaroid stack + heavy white caps is THE explainer card; black + white/red caps is THE punchline card.
5. Talker inside a rounded frame on paper with mono labels/timecode chip = the split-screen language.
6. Every card enters fast (3-6 frames) and leaves faster (2-3 frames) or via a push-through.
